What “MVP” should mean in the Gulf

For GCC startups, a useful MVP unlocks a concrete next decision: a pilot customer in Riyadh or Dubai, a fundraising proof point, or an operational bottleneck removed. It is not a smaller version of the entire vision, and it is not a list of every regional feature you might need later.

Scope before you hire

  • One sentence for the decision v1 must unlock
  • Three to seven capabilities; explicit non-goals
  • Language stance: Arabic-first, English-first, or bilingual
  • Platforms, payments path, and who owns store accounts
  • Who accepts work daily (founder, product lead, or both)

Use the scoping checklist before a studio or freelancer starts billing.

Regional product realities

  • Arabic UX / RTL — decide which surfaces need full RTL in v1; partial translation without layout ownership creates rework
  • Payments & subscriptions — name the rails and stores you can actually open now
  • Identity & messaging — pick providers that work for Gulf users
  • Compliance copy — privacy, terms, and store text are product work, not an afterthought

FAQ

Can a remote engineering partner work for a GCC startup?

Yes, when scope is written, communication cadence matches Gulf working days, and product decisions stay with the founder or local product owner. Zinova is Estonia-registered and delivers remotely without claiming a local Gulf office.

Should v1 be Arabic-first?

Only if your first users need it. Decide Arabic-first, English-first, or bilingual before build. RTL and copy ownership belong in scope, not as a late surprise.

What should we lock before hiring?

The decision the MVP must unlock, non-goals, platforms, payments path, language/UX, and who accepts work day to day. See also the scoping guide.
